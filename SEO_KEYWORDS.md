# Target keywords — what we're trying to show up for in Google

This is the list `CONTENT_GUIDE.md` (see its step 1) points back to when
picking a new blog topic, and what the homepage/blog copy should keep
naturally reflecting. English only (site language decision, see project
memory).

**How this actually works — no keyword ever gets "registered" anywhere.**
Google reads the real text on the page (title, headings, body copy) and
decides what a page is "about" from that. A phrase is "targeted" by
genuinely being covered in real content — a title, a heading, a
paragraph, an article - not by being listed in a hidden tag. (The old
`<meta name="keywords">` tag exists but Google has publicly confirmed it
ignores it - not used here.)

**On the brand name specifically:** "Scratch" alone will never rank for
us — MIT's Scratch (scratch.mit.edu, the children's visual-programming
tool) owns that word completely, for good reason (huge, decades-old,
massively linked site). The exact phrase **"Scratch for Traders"**
however has essentially zero competition — nobody else uses that exact
name — so brand-name searches for the full name are easy to win and
already covered (title tag, every page's `<title>`).

- ⚠️ = present but not the main focus of anything
- ❌ = not covered anywhere yet - a real gap
