How this week's lineup was chosen

Nearly 10,000 strategy variants were screened on the last 30 days of market data, and only a short list of the best-looking ones went on to a much stricter test. That second test replays every trade against real tick data instead of candle summaries, and it was unforgiving: fewer than half of the short list stayed profitable, and several that looked strong on the fast screen turned into losses. Only strategies that held up made it into the lineup. What you see on each card is the tick-precise result, not the fast screen's more flattering number.

Read the numbers for what they are

Every number on the page describes one fixed window of past market data, not your account, your broker, or where the market goes next. Backtests are best used to understand how a strategy behaves - how often it trades, how deep its worst stretch was, how much it needs the market to trend - before testing it on a demo account. If you want to see why that last step matters, read Backtesting vs. Demo Trading.
